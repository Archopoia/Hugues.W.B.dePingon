# How One Assumption Can Change an Entire World

## The Question

We like to think we see reality directly. But most of what we call "the world" is actually a model resting on a few assumptions we rarely examine. Which raises a question worth sitting with: how much would change if we altered just one of them?

## The Main Idea

My claim is that human worldviews balance on a small number of foundational assumptions, and that these assumptions are load-bearing. Change one, and the effects propagate outward - into culture, into what we bother to explore, into what we value, into what we can even imagine. The assumption is invisible precisely because everything is built on top of it.

I want to make this concrete with two thought experiments. Neither is a claim about what is true. Both are demonstrations of how much depends on the starting assumption.

## Example One: A Universe of Emptiness or a Universe of Fullness

Our current picture says the atom is mostly empty space - a tiny nucleus, a few distant electrons, and vast nothing between. From this we inherit a mood as much as a fact: matter is thin, the solid world is a kind of illusion, we are ghosts of mostly-void bumping into other ghosts of mostly-void.

Now change the assumption. Suppose instead that what we call "empty" is packed with structure we simply cannot perceive - that the gaps are full of something our instruments and senses do not register. I am not arguing this is correct. I am pointing at what happens to the *feeling* of the world when you swap the premise. A universe of emptiness and a universe of hidden fullness are the same measurements wearing two different souls. One invites a metaphysics of scarcity and illusion; the other, a metaphysics of density and concealment. The equations might barely change. The experienced world changes completely.

## Example Two: Alternative Cosmologies

Take an even older assumption: how light behaves as it travels. Our cosmology of an enormous universe, with Earth as a speck adrift in it, depends on light moving in the particular way we assume it does. Alter that assumption - imagine light bending, or the space it crosses being shaped differently - and a civilization running the same observations could arrive at a radically different cosmos. They might conclude that they sit near the center of a bounded, enclosing reality rather than lost at the edge of an unbounded one.

Again, the point is not "and therefore geocentrism was right." The point is that "a tiny Earth in a vast void" and "a central Earth in an enclosed whole" are not just different facts. They are different *places to live*. They breed different courage, different humility, different reasons to look up.

## The Explanation

Why does one assumption carry so much? Because a worldview is not a list of independent beliefs; it is a structure. The foundational assumptions are the load-bearing walls, and almost everything else - values, ambitions, myths, the reach of the imagination - leans on them. Swap a wall and the rooms rearrange themselves, often without anyone noticing which wall moved.

This is also why worldviews are so hard to argue with from inside. You cannot easily see the assumption you are standing on. It feels like the floor, not a choice.

## Conclusion

I am not asking anyone to adopt a new cosmology. I am asking a more useful question: how do our models of reality shape our action? Because if a single assumption can reorganize an entire experienced world, then examining our assumptions is not idle philosophy - it is one of the few ways to genuinely change what is possible for us.

We do not only live in the universe. We live in our assumptions about it. And those, at least, we are allowed to inspect.
